# How the Philippine market actually buys

Use this to explain *why* a recommendation fits. Platform shares and prices shift every
year; when a number matters to a decision, WebSearch for the current figure
(DataReportal "Digital 2026 Philippines" is the usual source) and cite it.

## The five truths that shape every plan

1. **Facebook is the internet for a large share of buyers.** Discovery, community,
   news, buying and customer service all happen inside Facebook and Messenger. The
   Philippines has consistently ranked among the heaviest social media users in the
   world. A business without an active Facebook page reads as not real.
2. **Trust is borrowed before it is earned.** People buy from a kakilala, from a
   group they belong to, or from a seller with visible "feedback" (customer photos,
   screenshots of chats, tagged reviews). New brands borrow trust from groups,
   admins, micro-influencers and existing customers.
3. **The sale closes in chat.** "HM po?" (how much), "PM sent", "avail po". Buyers
   expect a fast, warm Messenger/Viber reply with price, photo and options. Reply
   time is a conversion lever; a 5-minute reply beats a better ad. Posting the price
   publicly ("price in comments" or in the caption) reduces friction and the
   annoyance of "PM for price".
4. **Money moves on payday.** Most salaried workers are paid on the 15th and 30th
   ("kinsenas" and "katapusan"). Spend clusters in the 2-3 days after. December
   has 13th-month pay (legally due by Dec 24) and bonuses. OFW remittances support
   household spending in many provinces.
5. **Mobile, prepaid, price-conscious, value-hungry.** Most browse on Android phones,
   often on mobile data. Pages must be light. "Sulit" (worth it) beats "premium" for
   most segments; bundles, free shipping, freebies and installment terms move people.

## Payments and logistics (the conversion path)

- **E-wallets**: GCash is near-universal; Maya is second. Show a QR or number.
- **Bank transfer**: BDO, BPI, UnionBank, InstaPay/PESONet for higher tickets.
- **COD**: still expected for first purchases from unknown online sellers, and
  default on marketplaces. Costs: RTS (return-to-sender) risk; confirm by chat first.
- **Cards / BNPL**: matter for higher tickets (gadgets, furniture, courses):
  installment via credit card, Home Credit, BillEase, Atome, SPayLater.
- **Delivery**: Lalamove, Grab Express, Move It/Angkas for same-day metro;
  J&T, LBC, Flash, Ninja Van and marketplace logistics for province shipping.
  Island geography means shipping cost and time to Visayas/Mindanao is a real
  objection: state it up front.

## Regional differences worth naming

- **Metro Manila (NCR)**: crowded, ad costs higher, traffic makes "near me" and
  delivery radius decisive. Groups are per city/village/condo/subdivision.
- **Mega Cebu, Davao, Iloilo, Bacolod, CDO, Baguio**: strong local pride and
  local-language content (Cebuano/Bisaya, Hiligaynon, Ilocano) outperforms Tagalog.
  Local food bloggers and city "foodie" groups have outsized pull.
- **Provinces / towns**: the town's "Buy and Sell", "Updates" and LGU-adjacent
  groups reach almost everyone. Offline (tarpaulin, barangay fiestas, palengke,
  parish, tricycle terminals) is still a channel, not a nostalgia.
- **OFW / diaspora**: buyers abroad paying for family at home. Timezone-shifted
  posting, trust via remittance-style reassurance, gifting and property.

## Social classes and messaging

Marketers in PH still segment by socio-economic class (AB, C1/C2, D, E). Practically:

- **ABC1**: Instagram, TikTok, Google search, malls, Viber communities, LinkedIn
  for B2B. Care about convenience, quality, aesthetics, brand story.
- **C2/D (the mass market, the largest by volume)**: Facebook, TikTok, Messenger,
  Shopee/TikTok Shop, sachet-sized offers, installments, freebies, testimonials.
- Price per unit, payment flexibility and social proof matter more than brand.

## Language

- Taglish is the default online voice in Luzon. English-only reads as corporate or
  premium (fine for B2B and ABC1). Pure deep Tagalog reads as formal.
- Outside Tagalog-speaking areas, local language or English + local phrases.
- "Po/opo" signals respect, expected when addressing customers and group members.

## Culture that shows up in campaigns

- Family-first ("para sa pamilya"), bayanihan (community help), pasalubong
  culture, fiesta and celebration spending, faith (Simbang Gabi, Holy Week).
- Humor and "hugot" (relatable emotional) lines outperform polished ads.
- Supporting local ("support small business", "proudly Pinoy-made") resonates.
- "Hiya" (shame, social discomfort): people lurk rather than comment, so measure
  DMs and saves, not only comments. Make asking easy and low-pressure.
