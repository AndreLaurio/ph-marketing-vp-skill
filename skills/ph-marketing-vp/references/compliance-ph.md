# PH compliance checklist for marketing

Flag what applies to the business in the roadmap's compliance section. This is a
checklist, not legal advice: tell the user to confirm with the agency for anything
that carries penalties. Laws and fees change; WebSearch the agency page when a detail
drives a decision.

## Business registration (trust signal and legal base)

- DTI business name (sole prop) or SEC (corporation/partnership), Mayor's/business
  permit from the LGU, BIR registration (Certificate of Registration, official
  receipts/invoices). Showing "DTI/BIR registered" in posts and the page's About
  section is itself a trust lever in PH.

## Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967)

- Took effect Dec 2023; DTI said its rules became fully enforceable after the
  transition period ended in June 2025. IRR: Joint Administrative Order 24-03 (2024).
- Online merchants, e-retailers and e-marketplaces must disclose price, product
  description and condition, and the seller's contact details; have a way to handle
  complaints/returns; protect customer data. DTI's E-Commerce Bureau can issue
  compliance and takedown orders; platforms can share liability for illicit sellers.
- Pure consumer-to-consumer selling is outside its scope, but a business selling on
  Facebook is a business.
- Practical: post prices, have a returns/refund policy, a business contact, and
  respond to complaints.

## Sales promotions, giveaways, raffles (Consumer Act, RA 7394)

- Promos that offer prizes through a contest, game or raffle and use widespread
  communication (social media counts) generally need a **DTI sales promotion permit**
  (or FDA for food, drugs, cosmetics, devices), applied **at least 30 days** before
  the promo starts. Calling it a "giveaway" does not exempt it.
- Posts must show the permit line ("Per DTI Fair Trade Permit No. ___"), definite
  dates and mechanics. Raffle draws need a DTI representative.
- Generally not covered: discounts/price-off, free samples, buy-one-take-one style
  premiums given to all buyers, in-store promos (confirm with DTI).
- Practical default: prefer discounts, bundles and freebies-with-purchase over
  raffles for small businesses; if they want a giveaway, budget the 30-day lead time.

## FDA (food, supplements, cosmetics, drugs, devices)

- Products must be registered/notified with FDA before being sold and advertised.
- Food supplements must carry "Mahalagang Paalala: Ang [product] ay hindi gamot at
  hindi dapat gamiting panggamot sa anumang uri ng sakit" (No Approved Therapeutic
  Claims) in ads.
- No cure/treat/prevent claims for non-drugs; no "FDA approved" claims unless true
  and permitted; creators repeating claims count as advertising too.
- Home food sellers scaling up: LGU sanitary permit, then FDA License to Operate
  for processed food distribution.

## Data Privacy Act (RA 10173)

- Customer names, numbers, addresses, chat screenshots and photos are personal data.
- Get consent before posting customer feedback screenshots with names/faces; blur
  names and profile photos otherwise.
- Don't buy or share contact lists; get opt-in for SMS/Viber/email blasts; give a
  way to opt out. Secure order spreadsheets (not a public Google Sheet).

## Advertising standards

- Ad Standards Council (ASC) Code of Ethics governs truthful, fair advertising;
  certain regulated categories and media placements need ASC pre-clearance. Avoid
  unsubstantiated superlatives ("#1 in PH", "best") without proof.
- Disclose paid partnerships with influencers.
- Price claims: if "was ₱999, now ₱499", the ₱999 must have been real.

## Category-specific

- **Health services** (clinics, dentists, aesthetics): professional advertising
  rules from PRC/professional boards; before/after photos need consent; no
  guaranteed results.
- **Real estate**: DHSUD license to sell for projects; only PRC-licensed brokers/
  accredited salespersons should sell; no guaranteed-ROI claims.
- **Financial products / lending / investment**: SEC/BSP registration; "investment"
  offers to the public need SEC registration; unregistered "paluwagan-style"
  investment schemes are a red flag.
- **Alcohol, tobacco/vape**: age restrictions; vape advertising is restricted.
- **Education**: CHED/TESDA/DepEd accreditation claims must be true; no
  guaranteed-pass claims.

## Platform rules

- Meta: no fake accounts, no engagement bait ("comment YES to win" can be demoted),
  restricted categories need ad authorization. Marketplace has its own commerce policy.
- Group rules beat everything: follow each group's selling rules.
